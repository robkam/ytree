# AppState dispatch invariant coverage audit

## Findings

### medium — tests/test_appstate_contract_guard.py:1039
- **Evidence:** `_complete_invariants()` now derives every fixture dispatch surface id from `_complete_dispatch_surfaces()` and passes that full list to every invariant (`tests/test_appstate_contract_guard.py:1039-1052`). Since `_invariant()` gives every invariant the same `protected_fields` including `"field"` (`tests/test_appstate_contract_guard.py:180-194`), the valid fixture no longer exercises any meaningful invariant-to-surface specificity. The new docs/runtime negative tests remove the target surface from every invariant (`tests/test_appstate_contract_guard.py:3772-3800`, `tests/test_appstate_contract_guard.py:3953-3983`), but they do not encode the stricter intersection case required by the contract: one invariant lists the surface without protecting the field while another protects the field without listing the surface.
- **Impact:** The implementation currently enforces the contract correctly, but the fixture change weakens the test oracle and could let a future/alternate guard implementation pass by checking “field protected anywhere” and “surface listed anywhere” separately instead of requiring the same invariant to provide both. This misses the prompt acceptance criterion that tests remain focused and do not mask meaningful failures.
- **Concrete fix:** Keep the complete fixture valid without assigning every surface to every invariant. For example, assign the direct-write surfaces to one explicit fixture invariant (or a small representative mapping), leave the other invariants with narrower/representative `dispatch_surface_ids`, and add a regression where the target surface and owner field are split across different invariants so the test proves the same-invariant intersection requirement.
